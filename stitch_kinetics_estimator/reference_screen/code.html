The core equation

Chemical kinetics is the study of rates of chemical processes. It encompasses investigations of how different experimental conditions can influence the speed of a chemical reaction and yield information about the reaction's mechanism and transition states, as well as the construction of mathematical models that can describe the characteristics of a chemical reaction.

The fundamental construct for quantifying the progress of a simple reaction over time relies on defining a rate law. This law correlates the reaction rate with concentrations or pressures of reactants and constant parameters.

r = k [A]^m [B]^n Equation 1: General rate law

Where r is the reaction rate, k is the rate constant, and [A] and [B] are reactant concentrations. The exponents m and n represent the partial reaction orders.

Reaction order

The overall reaction order is the sum of the partial orders. For many common reactions in homogeneous systems, determining these orders empirically is the first step in establishing a robust kinetic model.

First Order

Rate depends linearly on only one reactant concentration.

Second Order

Rate depends on the square of one reactant, or two separate reactants.

Temperature and Arrhenius

The temperature dependence of reaction rates is typically modeled using the Arrhenius equation, which introduces the concept of activation energy.

k = A * e^(-E_a / RT) Equation 2: Arrhenius Equation
